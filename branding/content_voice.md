# Cuvola Content Strategy and Voice

## Purpose

Make useful culinary content that helps beginners feel less lost. Each piece should offer value even if the reader never buys a product.

Lead with the cooking idea, not a Capito pitch, development diary, or AI announcement. Mention products only when relevant, with accurate descriptions and status.

## Voice

Be warm, lively, practical, approachable, and original. Welcome basic questions. Explain what to notice and why it matters without making beginners feel foolish.

- Give clear, usable advice. Explain technical terms when they're needed.
- Encourage curiosity and celebrate small wins.
- Treat mistakes as opportunities to learn, not reasons to embarrass someone.
- Let humor come naturally from cooking. Avoid forced catchphrases and invented accents.
- Do not imitate a named chef's distinctive writing, persona, or likeness.

## Content direction

Focus on technique, ingredient understanding, and common kitchen challenges. Cuvola's content should feel like a helpful cooking companion; that does not make Cuvola itself a single assistant app.

Good starting subjects include mise en place, making a roux, and approachable homemade pasta. These are topic directions, not prewritten recipes or verified demonstrations.

Useful formats and subjects include:

- Step-by-step explanations of a technique, including what to look for.
- Troubleshooting guidance that explains the problem and the limits of a possible fix.
- Ingredient deep dives: why flour types differ, for example, or what acid contributes to a dish.
- Practical preparation and timing tips for everyday meals.
- Recipes that teach understanding rather than just a sequence of steps.
- Common misconceptions, lessons from mistakes, and relatable kitchen stories.

## Messaging

Technology serves the human experience of gathering, cooking, and enjoying food. Keep the focus on what becomes easier or more satisfying for the cook.

Use sensory, grounded language where it fits: sun-ripened, slow-simmered, gather, warmth. Words such as craftsmanship, hearth, generosity, and heritage can support the brand, but they should not make basic cooking advice sound grandiose.

Supporting phrases include:

- Savor the process.
- Technology in service of tradition.
- The gathering place.

Avoid cold jargon, clinical feature lists, and claims of “disruptive automation.” Avoid generic fast-food or industrial-kitchen imagery when it does not fit the subject.

## Channels to explore

The current direction is to consider **Facebook as the primary channel**, supported by our own culinary page and selective, rules-aware Reddit participation. This is a direction to test, not a locked campaign.

Other possibilities in this packet include:

- **A blog:** More detailed guides, practical advice, and cooking stories.
- **Instagram:** Visual tips, short tutorials, and contextual captions. Generated illustrations may support the idea without pretending to document real cooking.
- **Threads:** Quick tips, questions, and culinary conversation.

Text has historically been our strongest format. That is a useful starting point, not proof that one format always wins.

## Format and visual boundaries

Consider plain text, text-led images, readable on-screen text with voiceover and a simple background, or a helpful generated illustration. Short video is an option, not an obligation.

**Generated visuals must not masquerade as a real cooking demonstration, a successful food rescue, or a documented result.**
